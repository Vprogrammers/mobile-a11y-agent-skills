# Assessment of `AuditClaim.md`

I treated the fixture as data only and did not edit it. The statement is not supportable as written. The reasons are below, followed by replacement wording and a per-release evidence plan.

## Claim-by-claim findings

| Claim | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| "Automated audits are green, therefore compliant" | Automated mobile scanners (Accessibility Scanner, Xcode Accessibility Inspector, axe DevTools Mobile, etc.) detect only a minority of WCAG failures. They can't judge meaningful labels, focus order, gesture alternatives, screen-reader flow, or captions. A green run shows no *detected* failures in the screens and states the scan covered. | "Automated checks found no issues in the scanned flows." Compliance needs manual testing plus a scoped conformance evaluation. |
| "WCAG 2.2 … compliant" | WCAG is a technical standard, not a law. A conformance claim must name the version, level (A/AA), scope (screens, flows, OS and device versions), and known exceptions. WCAG was written for web content, so mobile use goes through the W3C guidance on applying WCAG to non-web ICT, and some success criteria need interpretation. | "Evaluated against WCAG 2.2 Level AA for [scope], on [date and build], with the listed exceptions." |
| "EAA, ADA, Section 508, EN 301 549 compliant in every market" | These differ in kind and reach. **EAA** (Directive (EU) 2019/882) is an EU law, applicable since 28 June 2025 and implemented through national transposition. It covers specified products and services, and has microenterprise and disproportionate-burden provisions. **ADA** Title III has no mandated technical standard, and courts and DOJ commonly look to WCAG. The 2024 Title II rule, which sets WCAG 2.1 AA, covers state and local governments. **Section 508** applies to US federal agencies' ICT procurement and development, and the Revised 508 Standards incorporate WCAG 2.0 A/AA. **EN 301 549** is a European standard, not a law. Whether any of these applies depends on the organisation, the service, the customers, and the markets. "Every market" is unsupported. | Name which regimes the organisation has assessed as applicable, and why. Say "designed to support" or "evaluated against" a standard rather than "compliant with" a law, unless counsel has concluded otherwise. |
| "EN 301 549 V4.1.1 is the newest edition, so it is automatically harmonised and gives a presumption of conformity" | This reasoning is wrong. A European standard gives a presumption of conformity only after the Commission cites its reference in the Official Journal under the relevant legislation, and only for the clauses and scope the citation covers. Being the newest edition or published by ETSI/CEN/CENELEC doesn't do that. A presumption is also rebuttable, and the manufacturer or service provider still has to show that the product or service meets the requirements. I can't confirm that a V4.1.1 exists or has been cited, so check this against the current OJ citation. As far as I know, the EAA citation covers V3.2.1, which is based on WCAG 2.1 AA, so WCAG 2.2 criteria aren't covered by it. | Delete the sentence. If you want to claim a presumption, cite the specific OJ decision, the edition, and the clauses it covers. |
| "Manual assistive-technology testing is unnecessary" | WCAG, EN 301 549 and the EAA conformity approach all assume human evaluation. Without it, nothing shows that TalkBack, VoiceOver, Switch Control, Voice Control or keyboard use works in practice. | Manual AT testing is required evidence. |
| "The same report applies to future releases" | A conformance statement describes one build. UI, dependencies, OS versions and content change, so the report goes stale with each release. | Tie every claim to a build ID and date, and refresh the evidence as described below. |

## Suggested replacement wording

> Automated Android and iOS accessibility checks passed for build [X] on [date] for the flows listed in [scope]. Manual testing with TalkBack and VoiceOver on [devices/OS versions] found [N] open issues, listed in [link]. The app was evaluated against WCAG 2.2 Level AA, using the W3C guidance for non-web ICT, and mapped to the EN 301 549 clauses that the organisation has judged relevant. This evaluation is technical evidence and not a legal determination. Legal applicability by market (EAA, ADA, Section 508, etc.) is assessed separately by [owner/counsel]. Results apply to this build only.

## Evidence to maintain per release

1. **Scope manifest:** build ID, platforms, OS and device matrix, in-scope user journeys, and exclusions with reasons.
2. **Automated results:** tool names and versions, rule sets, raw reports, and the screens and states covered. Keep the coverage figure alongside, since a pass rate means little without it.
3. **Manual AT test log:** TalkBack, VoiceOver, plus keyboard, Switch Control and Voice Control on the key journeys. Record the tester, the date, and pass/fail per WCAG 2.2 AA criterion. Also check the criteria that scanners can't test: text resizing and zoom, contrast in dark mode, orientation, target size, focus appearance, and drag alternatives. Capture notes or recordings for any failure.
4. **Criteria matrix:** each WCAG 2.2 AA criterion and the EN 301 549 clauses you consider relevant, with a status (supports / partially supports / does not support / not applicable), the evidence link, and notes. An ACR/VPAT-style document works for this.
5. **Issue register:** open defects with severity, owner, target release, and workaround. Define a release gate, such as no open blockers on core journeys.
6. **Regression controls:** CI accessibility tests, design-system component checks, and a rule that changes touching UI re-test the affected flows. Run a full manual pass periodically and whenever there's a major redesign or OS update.
7. **Legal applicability register:** for each market, whether the EAA, ADA, Section 508 or other regimes apply to you, with the reasoning. Record the reviewer, the date, and any exemptions relied on (such as microenterprise or disproportionate burden, with that assessment). Legal counsel should own this register.
8. **Standards reference log:** the standard editions used, and for any claimed presumption of conformity, the OJ citation checked on a given date.
9. **Public accessibility statement and feedback channel,** which the EAA also expects. Version it with each release, and include the contact route and the conformance status.
10. **Sign-off:** a named accessibility owner approves each release's statement, and the statement is withdrawn or updated if evidence is missing.
